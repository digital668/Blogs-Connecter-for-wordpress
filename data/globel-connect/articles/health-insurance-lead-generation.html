Quick answer: which lead type should your agency buy?

What is health insurance lead generation?

Health insurance lead generation is the process of finding people who are shopping for coverage and collecting their contact details and consent, so a licensed agent can follow up. You can buy leads from vendors or generate them through your own channels.

Buying leads vs generating your own

In health insurance lead generation, bought leads give you volume this week. Owned channels give you cheaper, more durable demand, but they take months to build. Most agencies blend both.

  • Referrals and partnerships: slow to build, usually the lowest cost per policy.
  • SEO and content: compounding traffic, but no results in the first few months.
  • Paid search and social: fast, but you carry the creative, landing page and compliance work.
  • Bought leads: fastest start, with quality that depends entirely on the vendor.

If you'd rather have a partner run your health insurance lead generation campaigns, a customized lead generation program can be scoped around your states, products and consent requirements.

Individual/ACA, Medicare and group leads differ

In health insurance lead generation, individual and ACA shoppers, Medicare-eligible seniors and employers are different buyers. Each comes with its own price levels and rules. Medicare leads carry extra marketing and consent scrutiny. Group leads are usually fewer, slower and priced higher, because the sales cycle is longer and the commission is larger. Don't compare prices across segments as if they were the same product.

Exclusive vs shared vs aged leads explained

Two things separate these lead types in health insurance lead generation: how many buyers get the same person, and how old the consent is. More buyers means more competition and a lower price. Older consent means a lower price and higher compliance risk.

Shared leads

Shared leads are sold to a set number of agents, often three to five. The first agent to reach the consumer usually wins, so speed-to-lead matters more than script quality.

They suit teams with a dialer, auto-text and agents ready to call in a minute or two. They frustrate part-time agents who call back after lunch.

Exclusive leads: what "exclusive" should mean

Exclusive should mean one buyer, one named seller on the consent, and no resale. Some vendors resell "exclusive" leads after 24 hours or after another set window. That turns them into shared leads at an exclusive price. Get the exclusivity definition and the resale ban in writing, not just on the sales page.

If you're buying exclusive health insurance leads, ask how the vendor proves each lead was sold once.

Live transfers and inbound calls

A live transfer connects a pre-screened shopper to your agent while they're still on the line. Pay-per-call buys inbound calls from ads. Both cost more, but they're built so that you reach a person.

The risks are dead air when your agents are busy and disputes over call length and qualification. Agree on a minimum call duration and what counts as billable before you start.

Aged leads

Aged leads are older records, usually priced under a few dollars. They fit three uses: high-volume dialing, long-term nurture, and SMS/email re-engagement where you hold valid consent.

The catch is stale consent. A form filled out months ago for a different seller may not support your call or text. Ask for the original timestamp and consent text before you load anything into a dialer.

Health insurance leads cost: typical ranges in 2026

Most published health insurance lead generation pricing runs from under $5 for aged records to about $95 for live transfers. Treat these as starting points. They come from sellers, they conflict with each other, and your own cost per issued policy is the only number that counts.

How the ranges were assembled

The ranges above reflect publicly posted vendor pricing guides, including Aged Lead Store's cost guide, cross-checked against other vendor blogs covering shared, exclusive and call-based pricing. We rounded to wide bands rather than averaging. ACA, under-65, Medicare Advantage and group leads are priced differently, and seasonality moves prices. None of these figures is independently audited.

Cost per lead vs cost per bound policy

Hidden costs

  • Dialer, texting and CRM fees per seat or per message.
  • Agent time spent on duplicates, wrong numbers and unqualified shoppers.
  • Returns you forgot to file inside the credit window.

TCPA compliant leads: what must agencies verify?

In health insurance lead generation, verify that every lead has documented consent. That record should name who may contact the consumer, by what method, and when consent was given. The standard holds regardless of what happened to the FCC's one-to-one consent rule.

What happened to the one-to-one consent rule?

The FCC adopted a rule that would have required consent to name a single seller. On January 24, 2025, the Eleventh Circuit vacated it, just before its January 27 effective date. Some vendor pages still describe it as in force, and that's wrong. For the underlying rules on calls and texts, see the FCC consumer guide on robocalls and texts.

Why named-seller consent still matters

Vacated doesn't mean risk-free. Carriers and IMOs often set their own contract requirements. Several states have their own telemarketing laws, and plaintiffs' firms still sue over vague "partners" consent language.

Do Not Call registry violations are a separate exposure, so scrub against the National Do Not Call Registry as well as your internal lists. Ask your compliance advisor what applies in your states.

What consent documentation to demand

  • A certificate token from a tool such as TrustedForm or Jornaya, retained for each lead.
  • Timestamp and IP address of submission.
  • The exact disclosure text the consumer saw, with your agency named or clearly identified.
  • The page URL and opt-in method for calls and texts.

Handle health details carefully. Where leads include health information, privacy obligations such as HIPAA may apply to your workflows, so confirm with counsel.

Lead quality checks before you scale

Test every health insurance lead generation vendor on duplicates, contact rate, invalid numbers and delivery speed before you commit budget. A small, structured pilot costs far less than a quarter of bad leads.

Duplicate rate and cross-vendor overlap audit

  1. Export 30 days of leads from every vendor, including aged lists.
  2. Normalize phone numbers (digits only) and lowercase emails.
  3. Match across vendors and against your existing customers.
  4. Calculate the share of each vendor's leads that appear elsewhere.

High overlap on "exclusive" leads is a sign of resale. Use the data to request credits.

Contact rate, invalid numbers and verification

Track the percentage of disconnected or wrong numbers, the share that reach a live person, and the minutes from form fill to delivery. Ask whether the vendor uses OTP phone verification and DNC scrubbing.

Set targets in the contract. Reasonable starting points to negotiate: delivery within seconds, and a clear credit for invalid numbers returned within a few days.

Run a two-week pilot

  1. Buy at least 300 leads per vendor, split evenly across days.
  2. Use the same agents, scripts and call windows for each vendor.
  3. Log contact rate, invalid rate, duplicates, quotes and applications.
  4. Set pass/fail thresholds before you start, such as an invalid rate you won't exceed.

Be honest about sample size. Three hundred leads won't produce a reliable close rate, so judge early on data quality and consent evidence, then extend the winners.

Vendor vetting checklist for health insurance lead generation companies

A good health insurance lead generation vendor answers every question below with documents, not slogans. If a vendor dodges, treat that as your answer.

Questions to ask on the sales call

  • Where does each lead originate: owned sites, affiliates, call center, or purchased data?
  • Can you show the consent language, with sellers named?
  • Do you retain TrustedForm or Jornaya-style certificates and share them per lead?
  • Do you resell exclusive leads after 24 hours or any other window?
  • How fast is delivery to my CRM or dialer?
  • Do you OTP-verify phone numbers and scrub against DNC?
  • Can you filter by state, product and carrier appointment?

Contract terms

  • Written definition of exclusivity and a resale ban.
  • Return and credit window for invalid, duplicate or out-of-state leads.
  • Consent records available within a set number of days on request.
  • Compliance indemnity and a right to audit.
  • No long minimum commitments before the pilot ends.

Red flags

  • Guaranteed policy counts or income claims.
  • Vague "marketing partners" consent language.
  • No certificate or timestamp on request.
  • Refusal to provide references or a sample batch.
  • Pressure to prepay for large volumes.

How to choose: decision matrix by agency size

For health insurance lead generation, pick based on how quickly and how often you can follow up. There's no single winner.

Agency profileLead mix to test
Solo agent, limited calling timeExclusive leads or inbound calls in small batches, plus referrals
5–20 agent agencyExclusive as the base, shared for peak days, aged for nurture
Call center with 200+ leads a dayShared and aged at volume, live transfers for closers, strict speed-to-lead tracking

Plan around seasonality, too. Annual and open enrollment periods push prices up and compress response windows. Lock in vendor terms and staffing before the spike, not during it.

Where outsourcing helps

Outsourcing part of your health insurance lead generation helps when leads arrive faster than your team can work them. A support partner can handle first-touch calls, appointment setting, list cleanup and back-office tasks, so your licensed agents can spend their time on quoting and enrollment.

  • Follow-up and appointment setting: an international call center team can cover extended hours for first contact.
  • Data cleanup: data entry services help dedupe, standardize and tag lead lists before dialing.
  • Finance and insurance campaigns: see our financial lead generation work.
