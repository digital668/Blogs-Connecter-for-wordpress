The most useful B2B lead generation examples are small and specific: one list, one channel, one offer and one agreed definition of a "qualified" lead. Most roundups show SaaS brands with big budgets. Below are nine setups for healthcare, education and finance businesses that a small team can run, with scripts, subject lines and the numbers to track.

One clarification first. These examples target business buyers: practice managers, hospital administrators, school and college decision-makers, CFOs and compliance heads. They aren't patient, student or consumer acquisition campaigns. Every number in these B2B lead generation examples is illustrative, not an industry benchmark.

The metrics that matter before you copy B2B lead generation examples

Track cost per qualified lead, not cost per lead. A cheap lead nobody can sell to is the most expensive kind. Decide the metrics and the lead definition before launch, so you aren't arguing about them after the money is spent. That applies to all nine B2B lead generation examples below.

Cost per lead vs cost per qualified lead

  • Cost per lead (CPL) = total campaign cost ÷ leads captured.
  • Cost per qualified lead (CPQL) = total campaign cost ÷ leads that meet your definition.

Here's an illustrative example. You spend $2,400 in a month on calling time, list data and tools. You capture 60 leads, and 24 meet your definition. CPL is $40. CPQL is $100. The second number tells you whether the campaign is affordable.

Qualification, contact, appointment and opportunity rates

  • Contact rate = conversations with the right person ÷ records dialled or emailed (300 ÷ 1,000 = 30%).
  • Appointment-set rate = meetings booked ÷ conversations (60 ÷ 300 = 20%).
  • Qualification rate = qualified leads ÷ total leads (24 ÷ 60 = 40%).
  • Lead-to-opportunity rate = opportunities opened ÷ leads (6 ÷ 60 = 10%).

These figures are made up to show the arithmetic. Your own first month is the only benchmark that matters.

What counts as a "qualified" lead?

A qualified lead is a named decision-maker or influencer at a business that fits your target profile, has a real need and has agreed to a next step. Write the definition down by sector:

  • Healthcare: right role, practice or facility size in range, a named problem such as billing backlog or records digitisation, and a booked call.
  • Education: a decision-maker or budget influence, a student-count range, a buying window such as the next academic term.
  • Finance: an authorised finance or compliance lead, a defined business size, and a current process that your offer would replace or improve.

Healthcare B2B lead generation examples

These healthcare B2B lead generation examples suit companies selling to clinics, hospitals and health systems: billing, records, staffing, software and back-office support. Decision-makers are busy and cautious, so lead with low-risk reviews or education, not pitches. For more on how we run this work, see our healthcare lead generation service page.

Example 1: Outbound calling to practice managers and clinic groups

  • Audience: practice managers, clinic administrators and billing leads at multi-provider practices. Build the list by specialty, provider count and region, with a verified direct or main line.
  • Channel and cadence: three call attempts over eight working days, one voicemail, two short follow-up emails.
  • Offer: a free 20-minute workflow review of intake or billing data handling.
  • Opener: "Hi [Name], this is [Rep] with [Company]. I'll be brief. We help multi-provider practices cut manual data entry in intake and claims follow-up. Did I catch you at an okay moment?"
  • Metrics: contact rate, appointment-set rate, CPQL.
  • If it underperforms: the front desk is probably screening your calls. Ask for "whoever owns billing operations" rather than a name, and call early morning or late afternoon.

Example 2: Landing page and webinar for hospital administrators

  • Audience: hospital administrators, operations directors and compliance managers.
  • Offer: a 45-minute webinar, "A records-team checklist before your next audit."
  • Registration page copy: a headline naming the problem, three bullets on what attendees will leave with, a short form (name, work email, organisation, role) and a line saying "Don't include patient information in any form field."
  • Cadence: invite emails three weeks, one week and one day out; a reminder an hour before; the recording and checklist within 24 hours; calls to attendees in the following three days.
  • Metrics: registration rate, show-up rate, attendee-to-qualified-conversation rate.
  • If it underperforms: weak show-up usually means a distant date, no calendar invite or a vague topic. Shorten the lead time and make the title a specific problem.

Example 3: LinkedIn outreach to department heads, IT and compliance leads

  • Audience: department heads, health IT managers and compliance officers at facilities in your target size range.
  • Sequence: day 1, a connection note with no pitch; day 3, a useful resource; day 8, a soft offer for a short call.
  • Connection note: "Hi [Name], I work with hospital records teams on digitisation projects. I'm connecting with peers in [region]. No pitch, just good to be in touch."
  • Day 8 message: "Last week I shared our records checklist. If a second pair of eyes on your current process would help, I can do a 20-minute walkthrough."
  • Metrics: acceptance rate, reply rate, meetings booked per 100 connections.
  • If it underperforms: narrow the title list and drop links from the first message.

Healthcare compliance notes

Keep protected health information (PHI) out of outreach and lead forms. Don't claim your service is "HIPAA certified" or "HIPAA compliant" unless you can substantiate it for your own situation. The HHS HIPAA guidance explains the basics. Get consent for any follow-up, and have counsel review your approach. This isn't legal advice.

Education B2B lead generation examples

These education B2B lead generation examples are for companies selling to colleges, schools and training institutes: edtech, admissions support, content, assessment and data services. Buying often follows the academic calendar, so timing matters as much as copy. Our education lead generation page covers the wider service.

Example 4: Email sequence to school and college decision-makers

  • Audience: principals, admissions directors, deans and IT heads. Use institution type, student-count range and region as filters.
  • Cadence: four emails on days 1, 4, 9 and 16.
  • Subject lines: "Where admissions inquiries go quiet"; "Quick question about [Institution]'s enrolment follow-up"; "A one-page checklist for your admissions team".
  • Email 1 body: "Hi [Name], many admissions teams lose inquiries between the first form and the first call. We help teams close that gap with structured follow-up. Would a one-page checklist be useful? Reply 'yes' and I'll send it."
  • Metrics: reply rate and positive-reply rate. Open rates are unreliable because of mail privacy features.
  • If it underperforms: shorten the body, ask one question only and check your sending domain setup.

Example 5: Outbound calling to book demos or pilot programmes

  • Audience: the same decision-makers, plus heads of department for specific tools.
  • Offer: a 30-minute demo or a four-week pilot in one department.
  • Opener: "Hi, [Rep] from [Company]. We run short pilots that help colleges reduce manual admissions data work. Are you the right person, or is it handled by the registrar's office?"
  • Gatekeeper line: "I'm following up on a note about admissions data workload. Who in your team handles that?"
  • Metrics: demo-booked rate, demo show rate, pilot-start rate.
  • If it underperforms: avoid exam weeks and peak admission dates, and make the pilot smaller.

Example 6: Landing page with a free resource or pilot offer

  • Audience: decision-makers reached through email, LinkedIn or paid search.
  • Copy blocks: a headline naming one problem, a subhead with the outcome in plain words, three bullets, one proof line you can substantiate, a form of five fields (name, work email, institution, role, student-count range) and a clear privacy link.
  • Metrics: visit-to-form rate, form-to-qualified rate, CPQL.
  • If it underperforms: remove form fields, match the page headline to the ad or email, and check that traffic isn't mostly students.

Education compliance note: don't collect student records in forms. Review the rules around student data and consent with counsel, such as FERPA guidance from the U.S. Department of Education.

Financial services B2B lead generation examples

These financial services B2B lead generation examples are for advisers, small lenders, fintechs and accounting or compliance vendors selling to business owners and finance heads. Trust and accuracy come first, so avoid promises. See our financial lead generation service for the broader approach.

Example 7: Cold-call script for finance heads and business owners

  • Audience: CFOs, finance managers and owners of businesses in a defined size band.
  • Cadence: four attempts across two weeks, one voicemail, one email.
  • Offer: a 15-minute review of month-end or reconciliation workflow.
  • Opener: "Hi [Name], [Rep] with [Company]. I'm calling about how small finance teams handle month-end reconciliation. Is that your area, or someone else's?"
  • Compliance-safe claims: say what you do, not what the prospect will earn or save. Avoid "guaranteed" and "risk-free."
  • Metrics: contact rate, appointment-set rate, CPQL.
  • If it underperforms: test a trigger line, such as a recent funding round or new regulation.

Example 8: Webinar on risk, compliance or cash flow with follow-up calls

  • Audience: finance and compliance heads at growing businesses.
  • Offer: a 40-minute session such as "Cash-flow visibility for a ten-person finance team," with a worksheet.
  • Funnel to track: registration → attendance → qualified conversation. Watch each ratio separately.
  • Follow-up: email the worksheet the same day, then call attendees within 72 hours and non-attendees with the recording.
  • If it underperforms: if registrations are fine but attendance is weak, fix reminders. If attendance is fine but conversations are weak, fix the follow-up offer.

Example 9: Email sequence to reactivate dormant leads

  • Audience: contacts in your CRM who went quiet 6–18 months ago and never opted out.
  • Cadence: three emails over ten days.
  • Subject lines: "Is this still on your list for this year?"; "Did priorities change at [Company]?"; "Should I close your file?"
  • Body: "Hi [Name], we spoke last year about [topic]. Has anything changed? If it's not a priority, tell me and I'll stop writing. If it is, I can send a short update."
  • Metrics: reactivation rate = contacts who reply with interest ÷ dormant contacts emailed.
  • If it underperforms: clean the list first. Bounced and outdated records are the usual cause.

Finance compliance notes

Keep claims truthful and non-promissory, and keep records of what you said and to whom. CAN-SPAM covers B2B email as well as consumer email, and calls may fall under DNC and TCPA rules. Check the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide and speak to counsel. This is general information, not legal advice.

B2B lead generation email examples that follow CAN-SPAM

A compliant B2B email is short, truthful and easy to opt out of. The FTC says CAN-SPAM requires accurate header information, non-deceptive subject lines, a valid physical postal address and a working opt-out that you honour within 10 business days.

These subject lines fit that standard:

  • "Question about your intake workflow" (healthcare)
  • "A one-page checklist for admissions teams" (education)
  • "Reconciliation: one quick question" (finance)

Short body template: "Hi [Name], I work with [role type] on [specific problem]. We recently helped teams like yours [factual, substantiated description]. Would a [short resource or 15-minute call] be useful? If not, just reply 'no' and I'll stop. [Name, company, postal address, unsubscribe link]."

For these B2B lead generation email examples, aim for 50–100 words, one question and no attachments in the first message. For wider tactics, our lead generation guide covers the basics.

A 30-day test plan

  1. Days 1–5: write the qualified-lead definition, pick one of the B2B lead generation examples and build a list of 300–500 verified records.
  2. Days 6–10: set up tracking, scripts or emails, and the landing page if needed. Get your compliance review done.
  3. Days 11–25: launch. Review contact and reply rates every Friday and change one thing at a time.
  4. Days 26–30: calculate CPL and CPQL, then decide whether to scale, change the offer or stop.

When does outsourcing calling or data work make sense?

Outsource when the work is repeatable, high-volume and measurable, such as calling, list cleaning and CRM updates. Keep in-house what depends on judgement and your brand. Outsourcing makes most sense when hiring and training would take longer than your 30-day test of any of the B2B lead generation examples that rely on calling.

Tasks that outsource well

Appointment-setting callsYou need volume and have no callerCalls need deep technical knowledge

Email deliverabilityYou lack domain and warm-up expertiseYou already have an owner for it
Landing page and webinar operationsYou need scheduling, reminders and reportingContent is highly specialised
CRM updates and back officeData entry piles up after callsPipeline decisions are made in the CRM

Our data entry services and data conversion services cover list cleaning and CRM updates, while the calling team covers first-touch outreach.

Tasks to keep in-house

Keep the offer, message approval, qualification definition and closing with your own team. A vendor can book the meeting, but you decide what a good lead looks like and who closes it.

Questions to ask a vendor

  • Can I hear call recordings, and what is your QA process?
  • Where does your data come from, and how is consent handled?
  • How do you define a qualified lead, and can I approve it in writing?
  • What does reporting look like, and how often?
  • What is your replacement policy for leads that don't meet the definition?
  • How do you handle DNC lists and privacy requirements for my sector?

If a vendor can't answer these plainly, keep looking.

Common mistakes and how to fix them

Most failed campaigns built on B2B lead generation examples share a few causes: the wrong list, a vague offer or a missing definition of "qualified." Fix the list and the offer before you change the channel.

SymptomLikely causeFix
Low contact rateWrong numbers, gatekeepers, bad call timesVerify data, ask for the role, test call windows
Leads aren't decision-makersTitle filters too broadTighten titles and add a qualifying question
Emails land in spamNew domain, long copy, poor listWarm up the domain, shorten copy, clean the list
Webinar no-showsDate too far out, weak remindersSend calendar invites, remind twice, send the recording
Leads don't convertOffer promises more than sales deliversAlign the offer with the sales conversation
